# Code of Conduct

## Our pledge

We pledge to make participation in FinishBit a harassment-free experience for everyone, regardless of age, body size, visible or invisible disability, ethnicity, sex characteristics, gender identity and expression, experience level, education, socio-economic status, nationality, personal appearance, race, caste, color, religion, or sexual identity and orientation.

We pledge to act and interact in ways that contribute to an open, welcoming, diverse, inclusive, and healthy community.

## Expected behavior

Examples of behavior that contributes to a positive community include:

- showing empathy and kindness toward other people;
- respecting differing opinions, viewpoints, and experiences;
- giving and gracefully accepting constructive feedback;
- accepting responsibility, apologizing to those affected by mistakes, and learning from the experience;
- focusing on what is best for the community and project.

Unacceptable behavior includes harassment, sexualized language or attention, personal or political attacks, trolling, deliberate intimidation, and publishing another person's private information without explicit permission.

## Scope

This policy applies in project spaces and when an individual is officially representing FinishBit in public. Project spaces include repositories, issue trackers, review threads, community discussions, and project events.

## Enforcement

Report abusive, harassing, or otherwise unacceptable behavior privately through the repository owner's private contact method on their GitHub profile. Do not use a public issue when doing so would expose the reporter or affected people.

Maintainers responsible for enforcement will review reports promptly and fairly, respect the reporter's privacy and safety, and avoid conflicts of interest. They may correct, edit, reject, or remove comments, commits, code, issues, and other contributions, or temporarily or permanently restrict participation when behavior is inappropriate, threatening, offensive, or harmful.

Enforcement decisions consider context, severity, repetition, impact, acknowledgement, and remediation. Retaliation against someone who reports an incident is itself a violation.
